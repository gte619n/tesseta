## BLUF

The privacy posture doc (`docs/requirements/privacy-and-compliance.md`) is **factually accurate about the code** on every technical claim checked (deny-by-default routing, KMS envelope encryption, sub-as-canonical-key, SQLCipher mirror, webhook sig-verify off) — but it is **stale in the one place that raises legal stakes**: it says a user-facing privacy policy is "(required)… currently consent is implicit" (§4), while a full privacy policy has in fact been **published** at `website/public/privacy.html` ("Last updated: July 28, 2026", canonical `https://tesseta.com/privacy`). That published policy makes three promises the system does not currently keep: users can "request deletion at any time" (no deletion capability or runbook exists — DATA-003), "you can only ever reach your own records" (meal photos sit in a **public-read GCS bucket**), and Gemini content "is not used to train third-party models" (true only on the paid tier — [Likely] but unverified). For a 1-user app this is exposure-zero; **the moment user #2 relies on the published policy, unkept written promises become the classic FTC-deceptive-practice / consumer-protection fact pattern** — worse than having no policy. Two factual corrections to the shared brief: (1) the app is **not** on Google Play (Firebase App Distribution only; `docs/reference/deployment.md:117`), so Play data-safety/health-declaration duties are trigger-gated, not live; (2) a privacy policy **does** exist — the gap is in-product surfacing (web app has zero privacy links; the Android About screen links to a dead **placeholder domain**) and delivery of the promised rights. Sharpest real regime for a US health app: **Washington My Health My Data Act** — no size threshold, private right of action, deletion "including backups". Largest pre-public-launch cost item: Google **restricted-scope verification + annual security assessment** once the OAuth app leaves Testing, because Fitbit-derived vitals verifiably flow into Gemini prompts.

No critical findings — calibrated to a single-user app whose only user is the operator. 8 findings: 2 high, 4 medium, 2 low. Every gap carries its trigger (user #2 vs. Play listing vs. public launch).

### COMP-001 — Published privacy policy promises rights and properties the system does not deliver
- **Severity:** high | **Confidence:** [Certain] on each factual mismatch; [Likely] on legal characterization
- **Evidence:**
  - Promise: `website/public/privacy.html` §7 — "**Delete** — remove individual records, or request deletion of your account and associated data… use the in-app controls or contact us"; §6 — "When… you request that your account be deleted, we remove the associated records."
  - Reality: no server-side account deletion or export exists (`docs/requirements/privacy-and-compliance.md:67-71`; DATA-003 grep confirmation); no in-app delete-account affordance (settings sweep, web + android); no manual deletion runbook in `docs/` — fulfilling a request today means hand-executing the 34-item checklist in `docs/audit/2026-09-13/domains/05-data-lifecycle.md` §Deletion checklist.
  - Promise: §5 — "you can only ever reach your own records." Reality: meal photos are served from a public-read bucket (`infra/scripts/bootstrap-gcp.sh:77-79`, `allUsers:objectViewer` — DATA-003 item 27): any holder of a URL reaches another user's health data unauthenticated.
  - Promise: §3 — Gemini content "is not used to train third-party models." Reality: contingent on paid-tier status, unverified (H1); accurate if paid [Likely].
  - Meanwhile the posture doc says the opposite thing — `privacy-and-compliance.md:62-63,92` still lists the privacy policy as an unbuilt "(required)" item. The two compliance documents contradict each other; neither is authoritative.
- **Impact:** With one user (the operator) there is no injured party and no live exposure. From user #2 on, the published policy is a representation others rely on; a deletion request that can't be honored, or a public photo URL, converts a hygiene gap into an unfair/deceptive-practice fact pattern — the exact hook regulators use against health apps [Likely; not legal advice per header].
- **Effort_days:** 0.5 (align the words: write the manual-deletion runbook referencing the DATA-003 checklist, soften §7 to reflect manual fulfillment SLA, fix or caveat §5's absolutism until the bucket is private, condition §3 on verified tier) + the real fixes tracked elsewhere (DATA-003 deletion job 3–5d; bucket privatization — see friction table).
- **Autonomy:** high for doc alignment; deletion job per DATA-003.
- **Null_option_cost:** zero until **user #2**; from then on every day widens the gap between a public promise and capability, and the policy's own "Last updated" date shows it predates the capability. **Trigger: user #2 (words-vs-capability), public launch (regulator-visible).**
- **Prompt:** "Reconcile Tesseta's two privacy documents: (1) update docs/requirements/privacy-and-compliance.md §4/§8 to state a policy IS published at website/public/privacy.html and cross-link it; (2) in privacy.html, change §7 deletion wording to 'email us; we fulfil deletion manually within 30 days' until DELETE /api/me ships, and add a §5 caveat that meal photos are currently served via unlisted public URLs (or better: privatize the bucket first and leave §5 as-is); (3) write docs/runbooks/account-deletion.md that walks the DATA-003 §Deletion-checklist by hand."
